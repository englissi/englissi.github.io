Understanding Korean Honorifics: The Definitive Beginner Guide

Honorifics (존댓말, Jondaetmal) form the backbone of Korean communication. Rather than simple politeness, they reflect social dynamics, age differences, and mutual respect.

The Core Speech Levels

Modern spoken Korean primarily relies on three speech levels in daily interactions:

Speech Level Korean Name When to Use
Formal Polite 하하십시오체 (-ㅂ니다/습니다) Presentations, broadcast news, military, customer service, formal business.
Informal Polite 해요체 (-아/어요) Daily conversation with acquaintances, coworkers, strangers, and elders.
Informal Casual 반말 (Panmal) Close friends of the same age, younger family members, or children.

Special Honorific Vocabulary

Beyond adding endings like '-요', Korean uses entirely distinct verbs and nouns when showing respect to someone of senior status:

To Eat: 먹다 (plain) → 드시다 / 잡수시다 (honorific)

To Sleep: 자다 (plain) → 주무시다 (honorific)

To Speak: 말하다 (plain) → 말씀하시다 (honorific)

Age: 나이 (plain) → 연세 (honorific)

Name: 이름 (plain) → 성함 (honorific)

A Golden Rule for Foreign Learners

When in doubt, always default to the 해요체 (-아/어요) style. It is friendly, polite, and universally accepted in nearly all social situations without sounding overly stiff or accidentally rude.
